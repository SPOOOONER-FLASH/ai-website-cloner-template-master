# FSB NA visual teardown — homepage + FSB 1138 relaunch

Date inspected: 2026-09-02

Official references:

- <https://www.fsbna.com/>
- <https://www.fsbna.com/innovation/relaunch-fsb-1138-dieter-rams>

This is a visual-language study, not permission to reuse FSB assets, copy, product geometry,
brand claims or designer attribution. Canton Hyland should borrow the editorial discipline
while proving a different advantage: manufacturing, product range and export engineering.

## 1. Homepage — module-by-module reading

### Header and navigation

The header is extremely quiet: four primary destinations, centred FSB mark, location/language,
search and menu. It does not compete with the photograph. The useful lesson is information
priority, not the exact layout: HYDE should keep Products and conversion routes visible, but
remove secondary visual noise around the main image.

### FSB 1138 hero

The hero isolates one Dieter Rams lever on a near-black gradient. The product silhouette,
surface finish and proportion carry the entire message; there is no decorative room scene or
bundle of unrelated hardware. Negative space is functional: it gives the headline and product
equal status and makes the metal readable.

**Transfer to HYDE:** use one real, identifiable model per hero. A black 564 MB, a satin 305
panic device or a lock-body macro can work. Do not build another nine-object AI showroom.

### Product-entry tiles

Locksets, bronze and Foster + Partners are presented as independent visual statements. Each
tile answers one question and uses a close product/category image rather than an aspirational
interior that could belong to any supplier.

**Transfer to HYDE:** category tiles should show the product family itself or its correct door
interface. Two hamburger cards should become one product macro plus one authentic factory
proof image.

### Welcome/editorial introduction

FSB inserts a calm text interval before more product imagery. This prevents a catalogue from
becoming an undifferentiated wall of pictures and lets a short positioning claim breathe.

**Transfer to HYDE:** factory facts should sit beside one piece of evidence. Avoid stacking
several claims over a generic generated scene.

### Collection overview and colour story

The collection imagery repeats controlled product forms. The Le Corbusier colour section has
a precise editorial purpose: finish and colour are the subject, not decoration.

**Transfer to HYDE:** when finish is the story, keep the model constant and vary only confirmed
finishes. When compatibility is the story, keep the finish constant and show only a confirmed
set. Never vary product family, finish and application in the same frame.

### Projects, About and Magazine

Architecture is used only where the claim is architectural use, company context or editorial
content. The project image is not presented as a product specification. The magazine image
has a named subject and article destination.

**Transfer to HYDE:** use real installation/project images for Applications and real factory
images for About/engineering. Do not use a materials showroom to represent customer contact,
downloads or engineering support.

## 2. FSB 1138 Dieter Rams relaunch — image sequence

### Opening text and first macro pair

The page starts with context, then two close views of the same handle. One view explains the
whole silhouette; the next explains the junction, radius and surface. Cropping changes, but
the object does not mutate.

### Wide product image

The wide image resets scale and confirms the product as a complete object after the macro
details. This is a reliable sequence for B2B hardware: whole product → functional detail →
application or variant.

### Rams portrait and quotation

The portrait is evidence for a named design-history claim. It is not generic authority stock
photography. HYDE must not imitate this with an invented engineer persona; its equivalent is
named factory process evidence, verified testing equipment or a documented product origin.

### Sustainability and engineering copy

Material and manufacturing statements are adjacent to controlled product photography. The
visual does not overclaim certification. HYDE should follow the same boundary: photographs
can show metal and manufacture, while grades, cycle life, standards and certificates require
structured source evidence.

### Variant grid

The grid shows one product family across deliberate variants and finishes. Repetition builds
recognition because viewpoint, scale and background stay controlled.

**Transfer to HYDE:** a range grid may show a single verified family (for example one handle
series) across source-confirmed finishes. It must not mix a hinge, cylinder, closer and random
lock body simply to fill nine squares.

### Final four-quadrant product view

The closing composition creates richness from four views of one coherent product, not four
unrelated products. This is the strongest reusable pattern for HYDE: one source model, four
information-bearing views, no invented room.

## 3. FSB visual grammar worth borrowing

1. One image, one claim.
2. Product first; environment only when it supplies relevant context.
3. Large negative space and calm hierarchy.
4. Controlled black, white and neutral-grey palette.
5. Straight grids, no decorative shadows, rounded cards or fake depth.
6. Macro crops reveal material and interface rather than hide defects.
7. A repeated model creates rhythm; unrelated products create doubt.
8. Whole product → detail → application → variant is a credible narrative sequence.
9. Architecture proves use; factory photography proves manufacture.
10. Named evidence is stronger than generic prestige language.

## 4. HYDE-specific translation

| FSB trust device | HYDE equivalent |
|---|---|
| Dieter Rams/design heritage | 25-year tooling and manufacturing continuity, only where evidenced |
| controlled lever macro | real lock, cylinder, panic device and handle macro from source photography |
| designer portrait | authentic worker, machine, assembly, inspection or showroom record |
| one family variant grid | one confirmed product series across verified finishes/configurations |
| architectural project | anonymised but real application, or a clearly labelled installation guide |
| service editorial | direct email/Alibaba path backed by concrete OEM/export capability |

## 5. Rejection checklist for future homepage images

- Any hole, fastener, keyway, spindle, latch or strike not present in the source product.
- Opposing door-side components shown on the same face.
- Mixed black, brass and stainless parts presented as one purchasable set.
- A hinge replacing the cylinder/escutcheon required to complete a lockset.
- A heavy overhead closer or workshop-scale mechanism inserted into a small-hardware kit.
- Generic interior, sample library or material board used to represent a service with no link.
- Repainted factory equipment, workers, line layout, screens, labels or stock that weakens E-E-A-T.
- Old oval logo hidden with a white rectangle, incomplete crescent, residual registered mark or
  a patch that overlaps the product.
- AI image treated as proof of dimensions, certification, finish code or compatibility.

## 6. Recommended homepage image system

Use four image roles, each with a different proof burden:

1. **Product authority:** exact single-model source or conservative edit.
2. **Factory proof:** authentic documentary photograph; removal of an obsolete logo is the
   only acceptable local reconstruction.
3. **Application:** real installation photograph, or a clearly labelled concept that waits for
   engineering compatibility approval.
4. **Range/finish:** repeated source-confirmed family photographed with controlled viewpoint.
