# HYDE exhibition-display visual spike — 2026-09-02

Four offline concepts generated from the client's 2026-09-02 exhibition-photo set and HYDE's own product images. None is wired into `public/images`, homepage data, or generated output.

## What the exhibition photos say

The strongest references are not the busiest booths. Their shared system is:

- **A buildable display object:** freestanding monolith, deep wall panel, or tabletop rack with visible thickness, base, fasteners, and mounting shadows.
- **One family per field:** black lever handles, satin handles, exit devices, hinges, and lock cases are visually separated. A panel reads as a product family, not a random hardware kit.
- **Architecture before decoration:** neutral walls, controlled spotlights, corrected verticals, and an uncluttered aisle make the hardware legible at a distance.
- **Two useful camera distances:** a human-height three-quarter booth view explains the system; a low oblique macro view proves material and engineering detail.
- **Editorial restraint:** the better stands use fewer products, larger spacing, and one finish per grouping. Tables, visitors, food, neighboring logos, and ceiling clutter weaken the frame.

The supplied competitor booths are composition and display-engineering references only. Their brands, attendance, claims, certifications, product geometry, and booth identity are not reusable HYDE evidence.

## Direction recommended for the homepage

Use **01 as the spatial hero direction**, then **04 as the engineering-detail counterpoint**. Use **02/03 lower on the page** to explain product-family breadth. Factory photography belongs on Company and must retain its real proportions; it should not be stretched into a homepage hero.

Before any concept becomes a site asset:

1. Compare every visible product feature against its first-party source.
2. Remove or replace any invented mounting/geometry.
3. Add HYDE typography and copy in HTML, not inside the generated bitmap.
4. Register provenance and build responsive WebP derivatives.
5. Keep the concept labeled as an editorial visualization; it proves neither trade-show attendance nor certification.
