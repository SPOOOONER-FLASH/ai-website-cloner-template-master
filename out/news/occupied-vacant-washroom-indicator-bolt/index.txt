33:["$","p","20",{"className":"mt-24 text-c1 text-ink-secondary","children":"An indicator bolt fails in use far more often at the keep than in the bolt. The keep is the part on the partition or pilaster that the bolt slides into, and on a cubicle it is screwed into a panel that is thinner and softer than a door frame. Two things go wrong. The first is sag: cubicle doors are hung on light hinges and used hard, and after a few months the door drops a few millimeters, the bolt no longer lines up with the keep, and people slam the door or force the knob to make it engage, which is when the knob breaks and the complaint arrives about the indicator. 34:["$","p","21",{"className":"mt-24 text-c1 text-ink-secondary","children":"Both are installation matters rather than product defects, and both are cheaper to prevent than to repair. Use fixings made for the panel material (through-bolts or inserts where the panel allows) rather than wood screws into an edge. And if a washroom has a history of broken indicators, look at how far the bolt is traveling into the keep before blaming the bolt."}]
