37:["$","p","15",{"children":"On a pair, one leaf is active: it carries the lock or latch and the handle, and it is the one people open. The other is inactive. The flush bolts go on the inactive leaf, one at the top and one at the bottom, set into its meeting edge, the vertical edge that meets the active leaf; the active leaf's latch engages a strike on that same edge. With both leaves closed the bolts cannot be seen from either face, and the levers can only be reached with the active leaf open, from the side its lock already controls. That is the whole security argument for a flush bolt."}]
38:["$","p","16",{"children":"Two mistakes come up often enough to name. A flush bolt on a single door is the wrong product: a single leaf is held by its lock against the frame, and a second point of security there is a surface bolt or a second lock. A drawing or photograph showing a flush bolt on the hinge edge or on a single door is showing a mistake, whoever published it."}]

3e:["$","p","22",{"children":"A flush bolt is mortised into the leaf edge: it needs a routed pocket, it is invisible when the pair is closed, and it can only be reached from between the leaves.
