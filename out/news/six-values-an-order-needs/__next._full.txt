27:["$","p","9",{"className":"mt-24 text-c1 text-ink-secondary","children":"Part of the reason orders arrive with gaps is that no single person holds all six. Knowing who does makes the missing one quick to get."}]
28:["$","p","10",{"className":"mt-24 text-c1 text-ink-secondary","children":"**Model** is usually the specifier's or buyer's choice. **Finish** comes from the architect or the client, often from a general note rather than the row. **Backset** belongs to the door: the door manufacturer knows it for new doors, and the existing door shows it on a replacement. **Centre distance** belongs to the lock case and the lever plate together, so it comes from whoever chose the lock body. **Handing** comes from the drawings — the door's swing and which side is \"outside\". **Door thickness** comes from the door schedule or the door maker."}]

It is to ask whoever owns that value, and to say so in the reply: \"backset to be confirmed by the door manufacturer\" is a line that gets answered."}]

2b:["$","p","13",{"className":"mt-24 text-c1 text-ink-secondary","children":"Every one of the six has a characteristic way of being measured wrong."}]
2c:["$","p","14",{"className":"mt-24 text-c1 text-ink-secondary","children":"**Backset** is measured to the case instead of to the face of the faceplate, which puts it a few millimetres out — enough to put a lever off-centre on its rose. **Centre distance** is confused with other distances called by the same name: on a pull handle, the P= figure is the distance between the two fixing points, which has nothing to do with the spindle-to-cylinder distance on a lock. **Door thickness** is taken at the edge of a panelled door rather than at the lock position, where the leaf may be thinner. **Handing** is read from the wrong side of the door, or in the wrong convention, as our article on handing explains. **Finish** is written as a word that two factories understand differently. And **model** is written without its suffix, so the function goes missing."}]

31:["$","p","19",{"className":"mt-24 text-c1 text-ink-secondary","children":"For more than a handful of doors, a spreadsheet beats prose. A value that is not yet known is written as \"TBC\" with the name of whoever will confirm it, never left blank. 34:["$","p","22",{"className":"mt-24 text-c1 text-ink-secondary","children":"The six values are written for the product they come from — a mortise lock — and not every product has all six. A tubular lever set has a backset but no centre distance, because the latch and the lever share one bored hole. A euro cylinder has neither; its equivalents are overall length and split. A pull handle has no backset at all, and its P= figure means something else entirely. The rule for those is simple: write \"n/a\" in the column, never leave it blank. A blank looks like a value somebody forgot; \"n/a\" says somebody checked."}]
