Door & Window Hardware

Everything a door needs, from the lever in the hand to the lock case inside the leaf: door hardware in American terms, architectural ironmongery in British ones. Nine families, one standard: every dimension written down, every part made to work with the ones around it.

Don’t know the model? Find by door type and material

Nine ways into the catalog

Each family opens onto its models and the figures that decide whether they fit. The complete catalog below compares them side by side.

  1. Lever handles48 modelsThe first handshake a building gives. Lever sets in stainless steel, brass and matte black.
  2. Panic exit devices44 modelsMade for the day nobody plans for. Push bars for escape and fire doors, single-point to multi-point.
  3. Lock cases51 modelsThe part inside the door that keeps its word. Mortise cases by backset and bolt.
  4. Door control7 modelsEvery door, closed at the right speed. Surface closers sized by leaf weight and width.
  5. Door hinges29 modelsCarrying the door, quietly, for years. Brass, stainless steel and steel hinges.
  6. Glass door hardware23 modelsHolding glass without hiding it. Patch fittings and pulls for frameless doors.
  7. Pull handles14 modelsWhat people reach for first. Grip, pull and concealed handles for entrance and sliding doors.
  8. Lock cylinders45 modelsOne key, the right doors. Profile and keyed cylinders, master-key systems included.
  9. Hardware accessories69 modelsThe small parts that finish the job. Viewers, stops, transfer devices, bolts, indicators and latches.
