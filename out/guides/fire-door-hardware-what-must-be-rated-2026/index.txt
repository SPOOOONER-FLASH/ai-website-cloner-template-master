26:["$","p","14",{"children":"EN 1634-1 is the European fire resistance test for door and shutter assemblies. A result is expressed as a period in minutes with a letter classification — E for integrity, EI for integrity plus insulation, EW for integrity plus radiation — so EI 30 and E 30 are different results for the same duration."}]
27:["$","p","15",{"children":"That distinction is the one most often lost in translation. Integrity means the assembly stops flame and hot gas passing. Insulation means it also limits the temperature rise on the unexposed face. A door specified as EI when only E was tested is a specification that has not been met."}]
28:["$","p","16",{"children":"UL 10C and NFPA 252 are the corresponding North American tests. UL 10C is the positive pressure test, and the detail worth knowing is the hose stream test: after the fire exposure, the assembly is hit with a pressurised water jet. It tests whether what survived the fire also survives the impact and thermal shock of firefighting, and it has no direct counterpart in EN 1634-1."}]
29:["$","p","17",{"children":"This is why an EN result and a UL listing are not translations of each other.
