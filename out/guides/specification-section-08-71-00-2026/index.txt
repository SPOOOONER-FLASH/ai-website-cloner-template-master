5a:["$","p","19",{"children":"A performance specification states what the product must achieve (a durability grade, a corrosion class, a fire classification, a maximum opening force) and lets any product that demonstrably meets it compete."}]
5b:["$","p","20",{"children":"The common hybrid, \"Manufacturer X model Y, or approved equal\", is only useful if the section also says what equal means. Without that, \"equal\" is decided during value engineering by comparing shared features rather than differences, which is the failure mode described on our submittal page."}]
5c:["$","p","21",{"children":"The stronger hybrid names the product and the two or three properties that made it the choice. Then a substitute can be assessed against something."}]
5d:["$","h2","part-3-the-clauses-installers-actually-need",{"id":"part-3-the-clauses-installers-actually-need","children":"Part 3: the clauses installers actually need"}]
5e:["$","p","23",{"children":"Mounting heights, stated once with units. Where a lever center is specified, give it in the units the site works in and say from what datum, above finished floor is the usual, and \"finished\" matters where a screed or a carpet is still to come."}]
5f:["$","p","24",{"children":"Adjustment sequencing. Door closers should be adjusted after the building's air handling is balanced, because air pressure changes the force a door needs. A closer set perfectly in an unpressurised building is a closer that will be complained about."}]
60:["$","p","25",{"children":"Fire door instruction. That no component of a fire-rated assembly may be substituted or drilled beyond the tested configuration, and that any variation requires evidence."}]
61:["$","p","26",{"children":"Handover. The key register, the operating instructions, and the list of what was actually installed against what was scheduled, because those two lists differ on every project and the difference is worth recording while somebody still remembers why."}]
62:["$","h2","the-reference-clause-and-how-to-cite-a-standard-so-it-binds",{"id":"the-reference-clause-and-how-to-cite-a-standard-so-it-binds","children":"The reference clause, and how to cite a standard so it binds"}]
63:["$","p","28",{"children":"Part 1 lists the standards the section relies on, and a sloppy reference clause is the most common way a specification becomes unenforceable without anybody noticing."}]
64:["$","p","29",{"children":"Cite the standard by its full designation, and say which part or classification is required. \"Comply with EN 1935\" is not a requirement, EN 1935 classifies hinges across eight fields, and a hinge of any grade complies with it. \"EN 1935 grade 13, corrosion grade 4, suitable for fire doors\" is a requirement, because a product either meets it or does not."}]
65:["$","p","30",{"children":"Say whether you mean the standard as published, or as amended. A dated reference freezes the requirement at a known text; an undated one moves with the standard, which is usually what a long project wants and occasionally is not."}]
66:["$","p","31",{"children":"Do not cite a standard you are not going to check. A section that names eleven standards and receives evidence for none of them has taught the supply chain that the reference list is decoration, and that lesson applies to the standards that mattered as well."}]
67:["$","p","32",{"children":"And do not cite a standard from the wrong market. EN 1634-1 and UL 10C test overlapping but different things, as our fire door page sets out, and a section that names both without saying which governs has asked for something no product holds."}]
68:["$","h2","mounting-heights-and-the-datum-that-decides-them",{"id":"mounting-heights-and-the-datum-that-decides-them","children":"Mounting heights, and the datum that decides them"}]
69:["$","p","34",{"children":"Heights belong in Part 3 because the installer is the reader. Two things have to be stated: the number, and what it is measured from."}]
6a:["$","p","35",{"children":"The datum is the part that goes wrong. \"Above finished floor\" means above the floor as it will finally exist, after the screed, after the tile, after the carpet. On a project where the hardware is installed before the floor finish, a height set from the slab is wrong by the thickness of whatever arrives later, and it is wrong consistently across every door in the building."}]
6b:["$","p","36",{"children":"Common practice puts a lever center around 1050mm (41-3/8\") above finished floor, with exit device push bars and pull handles falling in a band roughly between 900mm (35-7/16\") and 1100mm (43-5/16\") depending on the device and the jurisdiction. Treat those as a starting point rather than a requirement: accessibility rules set the binding figures and they differ by country, so confirm the number that applies to the building before writing it."}]
6c:["$","p","37",{"children":"Where a project is bound by an accessibility standard, cite the standard in Part 1 and repeat the resulting height in Part 3. The installer works from Part 3 and will not read the reference list, and the reference list is what makes the height defensible."}]
6d:["$","h2","revisions-and-the-one-habit-that-prevents-the-worst-arguments",{"id":"revisions-and-the-one-habit-that-prevents-the-worst-arguments","children":"Revisions, and the one habit that prevents the worst arguments"}]
6e:["$","p","39",{"children":"A hardware section is revised, by addendum at tender, by instruction during construction, and by substitution after award. Each revision is a chance for two parties to end up holding different documents."}]
6f:["$","p","40",{"children":"Give every issue a revision number and a date, put both in the header of the schedule as well as the section, and state in Part 1 that only the latest issue governs. It sounds like bureaucracy and it is the cheapest dispute prevention in the package."}]
70:["$","p","41",{"children":"The argument it prevents is specific and common: a set changed by addendum, the addendum reaching the contractor but not the supplier, and forty openings arriving to the superseded set. Nobody in that sequence did anything unreasonable, and the cost lands on whoever cannot prove which issue they were working to."}]
71:["$","p","42",{"children":"Keep the superseded issues rather than overwriting them. When the question arrives eighteen months later about why an opening has the hardware it has, the answer is in the version that was current when it was ordered."}]
72:["$","h2","what-we-need-to-quote-a-section-without-asking-questions",{"id":"what-we-need-to-quote-a-section-without-asking-questions","children":"What we need to quote a section without asking questions"}]

A supplier who quotes a performance specification without holding the evidence has not won the package; they have deferred the conversation to the submittal stage, where it costs everybody more."}]
