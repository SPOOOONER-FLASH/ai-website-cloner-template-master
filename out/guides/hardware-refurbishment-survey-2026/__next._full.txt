26:["$","h2","the-sampling-rule-by-type-not-by-floor",{"id":"the-sampling-rule-by-type-not-by-floor","children":"The sampling rule: by type, not by floor"}]
27:["$","p","9",{"children":"You cannot open four hundred locks. You do not need to. But the common shortcut — sample one floor thoroughly and assume the rest — is the wrong axis, because buildings are not consistent by floor. They are consistent by **procurement batch**, and a batch usually maps to a door type: all the flat entrance doors, all the riser cupboards, all the office doors on the refurbished wing."}]
28:["$","p","10",{"children":"So group by type first, using the fields you can see: leaf material, lock type, forend shape, lever family, closer present. Then open a sample within each type, on more than one floor, including at least one door that looks slightly different from its group."}]
29:["$","p","11",{"children":"That last one is where the surprises are. A group of forty identical-looking doors usually contains three that were replaced separately, and the three are the ones that will not accept the hardware you ordered for the forty."}]
2a:["$","p","12",{"children":"Record which doors were opened and which were inferred. A survey that does not distinguish measured rows from assumed rows will be read as if every row were measured, and the assumed rows are exactly the ones that generate the reorder."}]
2b:["$","h2","photographs-three-per-leaf-and-what-each-must-show",{"id":"photographs-three-per-leaf-and-what-each-must-show","children":"Photographs: three per leaf, and what each must show"}]
2c:["$","p","14",{"children":"**The leaf, whole, from the pull side**, with the ID visible. This is the row's anchor and the thing that settles arguments about which door anybody meant."}]
2d:["$","p","15",{"children":"**The lock edge**, close, showing the forend and the frame keep together. This one image carries the forend shape, the keep type and the gap, and it is the image most often missing."}]
2e:["$","p","16",{"children":"**The face around the furniture**, showing escutcheon, rose, lever and any wear pattern, with a scale in frame — a steel rule is fine. A photograph without a scale records appearance and not dimension."}]
